# HexGroebnerTheory

The Mathlib companion for `HexGroebner`. Its governing contract is
[SPEC/Libraries/hex-groebner.md](../SPEC/Libraries/hex-groebner.md).

`Order` transports every lawful `Hex.IsMonomialOrder` into Mathlib's
`MonomialOrder (Fin n)` through `HexMvPolyTheory.monoEquiv`. It proves exact
strict and non-strict comparison agreement, leading monomial and coefficient
agreement for nonzero polynomials, and pointwise divisibility and LCM
correspondence. The construction carries the supplied well-foundedness and
multiplicative compatibility laws; it introduces no independent polynomial
interpretation.

`Examples` gives ordinary-kernel checks for lex, grlex and grevlex. Build with
`lake build HexGroebnerTheory +HexGroebnerTheory.Examples`.

`Certificate` proves `checkDerivation_sound` for direct shared positive
membership derivations. `Member` proves `checkMember_sound` for direct
derivations and composed forward-containment/cofactor certificates. `MemberExample` replays a literal six-node witness and proves
`y²−x` belongs to the ideal generated by `x²−y` and `xy−1` over 𝔽₇.
Malformed nodes, forward references, noncanonical factors and wrong identities
are rejected by the Mathlib-free checker.

`CertificateLeading` proves that the certificate list scan selects the actual
leading exponent and coefficient under all three explicit orders. It uses the
existing residue-polynomial correspondence; canonical arity, unique exponents,
nonzero coefficients and residue bounds are hypotheses. `Examples` also checks
literal residue lists through this bridge using ordinary kernel reduction.
`CertificatePair` proves that exact list S-polynomials denote Mathlib's
S-polynomials, including nonmonic leading coefficients and zero inputs.
`CertificateReduction` proves that accepted cofactor identities with checked
product-degree bounds are zero-remainder representations in Mathlib.
`Examples` replays a literal final S-pair obligation; the executable tests reject
an exact ideal-membership identity whose products exceed the required bound.
`CertificateContainment` proves shared ideal-containment replay sound.
`CertificateShape` proves monicity, reducedness and decreasing leading order.
`CertificateBasis` combines those results with all final S-pair obligations and
Buchberger's criterion to prove `checkBasis_sound`. The postcondition is the
reduced Gröbner basis of the original ideal, without producer provenance.
`Examples.certificate_basis` proves a complete three-polynomial basis over 𝔽₇
by ordinary kernel replay of both containments and all nine final pair identities.
`checkBasis_unique` combines acceptance with reduced-basis uniqueness, so two
accepted outputs for the same original ideal agree as formal polynomial sets.

`Groebner` states the basis predicate by leading-monomial divisibility and
proves its equivalence with the leading-term-ideal predicate. It supplies
Buchberger's criterion using bounded zero-remainder representations, and unique
remainders. `Reduced` proves uniqueness of reduced Gröbner bases of an ideal.

`Upstream/Remainder` and `Upstream/Groebner` adapt the open
[Mathlib PR #29203](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/pull/29203)
by Junyu Guo and Hao Shen, at revision
`cb459855cbe79d8550c86a11220b65ab517810a5`. Original copyright and attribution
are preserved. The adapters use pinned Mathlib's division results and current
lemma names. These adapters retain upstream declaration names. A Mathlib pin
update supplying those declarations must remove the local copies and import
upstream modules in the same change, avoiding duplicate declarations.

`CertificateDivision` proves independent division replay sound, including
explicit quotient reconstruction, Mathlib's standard degree bounds and full
remainder irreducibility without a Gröbner-basis assumption. `Division` gives
`divideWith_sound` for sealed accepted results. `CertificateNormalForm` proves
`checkNonmember_sound` and `normalForm_spec`: the accepted remainder is the
unique irreducible representative of the original ideal coset, including
comparison with arbitrary representatives. `NormalForm` and `Membership`
connect those results to the bounded checked APIs. Positive results include
a standalone composed positive certificate against the original generators,
accepted by `checkMember`; their original-generator theorem follows directly
from `checkMember_sound`. `DivisionExample` replays
ordinary characteristic-two nonmembership and normal-form examples in the
kernel. `Audit` checks the new theorems use only Lean's standard foundational
axioms.

`Basis` proves `basisWith_sound` and `certifyWith_sound` for the exact original
canonical inputs and supplied candidate. Their receipts carry independent
acceptance, with no producer-provenance hypothesis. `GeneratedExample` replays
a literal certificate produced by traced F4 and automatic cofactor generation
in the ordinary kernel. Its axiom audit uses the standard three axioms.
[`PolynomialExample`](PolynomialExample.lean) derives a nonmembership statement
written directly with Mathlib polynomials and an ideal generated by two explicit
polynomials; certificate transport stays inside the proof.

Deterministic F4 correctness and termination remain required by the same
contract. Pure
`checkMember`, `checkBasis` and `checkNonmember` predicates have no operational
allowance. Division and complete-basis verification provide separate bounded
wrappers with cumulative nested-work reservations and sealed resumption.
S-pair witnesses currently use explicit cofactor arrays; compression and
efficient shared S-pair derivations remain required. The executable F4
candidate API remains unchecked.

The producer's pair-coverage proof also requires Buchberger's strict-LCM
standard-representation criterion and closure under the installation measure
(LCM, maximum basis index, representative priority). `buchberger_iff` instead
states the full zero-remainder criterion, bounded by the S-polynomial degree.
`spoly_chain` and `lcm_lt` provide the exact chain identity and proper-divisor
comparison; they do not by themselves complete the producer invariant.
