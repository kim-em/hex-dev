## Accomplished

Wrote [SPEC/Libraries/hex-sparse-poly.md](../SPEC/Libraries/hex-sparse-poly.md),
expanding the "Sparse univariate polynomials" bullet of
[SPEC/future-work.md](../SPEC/future-work.md) into a full SPEC for
`hex-sparse-poly` and its companion `hex-sparse-poly-mathlib`.

The design decisions the SPEC records:

- Representation is `Array (Nat × R)` with `SparsePolyCanonical` bundled
  as a proof field: strictly increasing exponents (which forbids
  duplicates and unsortedness in one condition) and no stored zeros. The
  SPEC enumerates every operation that can produce a cancelled or
  duplicated term, including the derivative over `ZMod p` and
  `substPow 0`, since those are where an implementation forgets to
  canonicalise.
- `addTerm` is the single place the invariant is established, and
  `ofTerms` is a fold of it. That makes `coeff_ofTerms` need only a
  left-identity law for `0`, and confines the associativity and
  commutativity requirement to the `@[csimp]` sort-and-combine twin.
- Multiplication's specification is `ofTerms` of the pairwise products
  (kernel-facing, per design principle 11), with three candidate
  implementations behind a `@[csimp]` equality and a bench family that
  picks between them.
- Round trips are stated twice: unconditionally on the bundled types, and
  at the array level with the canonicality hypothesis each direction
  needs, together with a counterexample for each half of each invariant.
- Gcd and division convert through `DensePoly`, with an explicit
  non-promise about remainder sequences staying sparse and a bench family
  ("convert-gcd") that measures the conversion share before anyone
  commits to a sparse division algorithm.
- No `PolyOps` / `LawfulPolyOps`, with the reasons in their own section.
- Cyclotomic sparse output stays a downstream adapter; nothing in the
  library knows what a cyclotomic polynomial is.

## Second-opinion revisions

A Codex review of the first draft returned 16 findings, all of which
checked out against the source and all of which are addressed. The five
that changed the design rather than the prose:

- **Design principle 11 was violated.** `addTerm`, `coeff`, and
  `ofDense` were specified as array/binary-search operations *and* placed
  in the `decide` closure, which the principle forbids. They are now
  `noncomputable` `List`-shaped specifications with `@[csimp]` twins,
  matching `DensePoly.eval` / `compose` / `derivative`.
- **The stable-sort algebra was wrong.** A stable sort preserves order
  within an equal-exponent block, so the combining pass computes the same
  left fold and needs only a left-identity law. The draft demanded
  associativity and commutativity. Stability is now stated as a
  requirement on the implementation, with `List.foldl_add_perm` named as
  what an unstable sort would need instead.
- **The Euclidean contracts were absent or over-promised.** The dense
  library has no unconditional monic-division identity (it routes through
  `divModMonic_eq_divMod_of_monic`), so the transported statements carry
  `DivModLaws` and `GcdLaws` rather than inventing weaker hypotheses.
  `degree?_toDense`, `leadingCoeff_toDense`, and `monic_toDense` were
  missing and are what actually carry monicity across.
- **"Exact division by a monomial is `mulMonomial` with a negative
  shift" was false** with `Nat` exponents. Replaced by a checked
  `divMonomial?`.
- **The encapsulation rule had no iteration API.** `toTerms` and
  `foldTerms` added, as [hex-mv-poly](../HexMvPoly/SPEC/hex-mv-poly.md)
  has.

Four factual errors were corrected: `Φ_p` has `p` nonzero coefficients
and not `p − 1` (the same slip is in `SPEC/future-work.md`, fixed there
too); the `x^n − 1`, `x^m − 1` pair was cited as a *dense* remainder
sequence when it is the standard example of one that stays sparse;
`deriving DecidableEq` does not fail on a proof-field structure on this
toolchain, so the reason to hand-write the instance is the derived-`decEq`
module-boundary gap instead; and `HexPolyMathlib.equiv` is stated at
`[Semiring R]`, not `[CommRing R]`.
