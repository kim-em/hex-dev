# 2026-08-03 — issue #9142: a subquadratic dense polynomial product for the Hensel lift

## Accomplished

**Measured before choosing.** A throwaway probe compared thresholded Karatsuba
against Kronecker substitution on the shapes the lift issues. The first
measurement was wrong and looked plausible: the timing loop's body is
loop-invariant, and the compiler hoisted it, so schoolbook appeared to cost
~9 µs at degree 90 and barely scale with degree. Threading the running checksum
through the choice of operand fixed it. The corrected numbers put schoolbook at
1.19 ms for a degree-90, 92-bit product, Karatsuba (best base case 16) at about
half that, and Kronecker at an eighth. Kronecker was the only candidate that
could reach the issue's 20% lift target, so Karatsuba was dropped.

**Kronecker substitution, proved.** `HexPolyZ/Kronecker.lean`:

- `packAux` / `unpackAux`: balanced divide-and-conquer packing and base-`2^b`
  digit extraction, `O(n log n)` limb operations rather than the `O(n²)` a
  sequential Horner scan pays.
- `constPack`: the constant bias repunit by doubling. The calibration probe
  showed a general `packAux` spent 53 µs of the then-196 µs kernel on this one
  constant; doubling took the kernel to 149 µs.
- Signed coefficients are carried by a per-slot bias of `2^(b-1)`, so digit
  extraction stays plain division with no borrow propagation.
- `mulKroneckerAt_eq` proves the kernel equal to the schoolbook `DensePoly.mul`
  on all inputs, via `kronecker_identity` (stated with the no-overlap bound as
  a hypothesis) plus a runtime coefficient scan (`maxAbs`) that discharges that
  bound on every call. No `sorry`, no new axioms.

**`eval_mul_commring`.** The substitution needs Horner evaluation to be
multiplicative, which the library did not have. `HexPoly/Euclid/EvalMul.lean`
proves it by peeling the top coefficient off the left factor, reusing the
existing `mul_add_left_poly` / `monomial_one_mul_poly_eq_shift` / `scale_mul`.
`eval_scale_semiring` and `eval_shift_semiring` fall out and are exported.

**Wiring.** `factorError` and `mulModSquare` in `HexHensel/Quadratic.lean` get
`@[csimp]` implementations that take the product by Kronecker. Between them
they cover all nine polynomial products of the bignum quadratic step. Both
specifications are untouched, so every existing proof about them is untouched.
`mulModSquare` had to stop being `private`, because `csimp` lemmas must be
public.

## Current frontier

Two cutoffs, not one. A size-only cutoff regresses small-coefficient rows.
Schoolbook cost per coefficient pair steps *twice* as coefficients widen —
about 9 ns up to 12 bits, about 53 ns at 16, about 120 ns at 20 — and Kronecker
only amortises against the widest regime at degree 24. The first committed pair
(24, 16) was read off a coarser grid that put the 16-bit crossover at degree 24;
the recorded sweep puts it at 32, which would have been a 27% regression on
16-bit coefficients at degrees 24 to 31. The cutoffs are `kroneckerSizeCutoff =
24` and `kroneckerBitCutoff = 20`, both read off
`scripts/bench/kronecker_crossover.py`. The lift is unaffected by the second
revision: its bignum step only runs once `m * m` exceeds a machine word, so its
coefficients are never narrower than 64 bits.

## Next step

`wilkinson_56` is a measured no-go, which #9142 allows for. It factors into 56
linear factors, so its lift is a product tree whose nodes multiply operands of
2 to 29 coefficients; only the root clears the size cutoff. Its lift time
improves by 6%, not 20%. A width-indexed size cutoff would reach further down
that tree — the sweep already shows 20-bit-and-wider coefficients ahead at 16
slots — and is the obvious next measurement.

The x-power and low-coefficient rows leave a win on the table too: at 4-bit
coefficients Kronecker pulls ahead past degree 90, but the bit cutoff keeps
that band on schoolbook because degree 64 in it still regresses.

`unpackAux` is now the largest remaining cost in the kernel (50 µs of 149 µs at
degree 90). It is dominated by big-integer allocation, three per internal node,
plus two array allocations and an append per node; filling one preallocated
array by index would remove the latter.

A second-opinion review (Codex) raised two more, both recorded in the SPEC and
neither a correctness issue: the slot width budgets `A²` for the larger of the
two coefficient bounds where `maxAbs p * maxAbs q` would do, and the crossover
benchmark's anti-hoisting branch was semantically constant — the checksum is now
clock-seeded so a stronger optimiser cannot fold it away. The same review
confirmed from the generated C that the bignum quadratic step routes through
exactly one `factorErrorImpl` and eight `mulModSquareImpl` calls, and that the
monomial division path still uses its shift-and-scale kernel.
