# Draft the `hex-mv-poly` SPEC

## Accomplished

- Wrote `SPEC/Libraries/hex-mv-poly.md`: a Mathlib-free multivariate
  polynomial library keyed on exponent vectors, with `Std.ExtTreeMap`
  storage, the monomial order as an explicit argument, and a companion
  supplying `aeval` and the ring equivalence with
  `MvPolynomial (Fin n) R`.
- Settled the representation question with measurement rather than
  folklore: `ExtTreeMap` does reduce under `decide +kernel`, contrary to
  the received view. The SPEC records what that experiment does and does
  not establish, and defers the comparative claim to a Phase 4 bench with
  a threshold fixed in advance.
- Audited the consumer surfaces from source rather than by eye:
  `leanprover/sos`, and CompPoly's public multivariate declarations as a
  capability checklist.

## Current frontier

- The SPEC is complete as a design. Three questions are deliberately left
  to evidence the SPEC itself schedules: kernel-side `ℚ`, the
  representation bench, and whether CompPoly's Horner grouping is worth
  porting.
- Not yet registered in `libraries.yml`; that is the decision to start
  implementation.

## Next step

- First implementation milestone is a stub `MvPoly` with `sorry`-ed
  proofs that `sos` and CompPoly's two `CMvEquiv` files build against,
  which converts the call-surface audit into real evidence.

## Blockers

- None.
