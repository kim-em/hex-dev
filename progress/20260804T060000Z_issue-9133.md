# 2026-08-04 — issue #9133, iterated quadratic norm irreducibility certificates

## Accomplished

The go/no-go investigation, a prototype outside production, and the verdict:
**go**, on both the mathematics and the measurements.

The class is iterated quadratic norms. For `d : ℤ` and monic `g`,
`N_d(g) = g(X - t) * g(X + t)` in `ℤ[t]/(t² - d)`, and iterating from `X - c`
gives `∏_ε (X - c - ∑ εᵢ √dᵢ)`. Swinnerton-Dyer rows are the case
`c = 0`, `dᵢ` the first `n` primes, but nothing in the construction asks for that.

The theorem: if no nonempty subproduct of the radicands is a perfect square --
which is exactly independence in `ℚ*/(ℚ*)²` -- then `[ℚ(√d₁,…,√dₙ):ℚ] = 2ⁿ`,
`c + ∑√dᵢ` generates that field, and the iterated norm is its minimal
polynomial, hence irreducible. The proof needs no Kummer theory: an elementary
lemma (a square in `F(√a)` that lies in `F` makes `b` or `ab` a square in `F`)
plus one subset induction gives the degree, and a trivial-stabilizer argument
gives the primitive element. `reports/hexbz-quadratic-norm-certificate.md` has it
statement for statement.

The certificate is the translation and the radicands, checked by `2ⁿ - 1`
integer square tests and one coefficient equality. No number field is
constructed, nothing is factored, no floating point. The recovery side --
untrusted, since the check is exact -- has a closed form: `∑_ε exp(t α_ε) =
2ⁿ ∏ᵢ cosh(t √dᵢ)`, so taking logs of the power-sum series recovers `∑ᵢ dᵢᵏ` and
Newton's identities recover the radicands, all from the top `2n + 1`
coefficients.

Measured on `chungus2`, paired in-process against the production cascade:

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `sd5` | 68.853 ms | 83.7 us | 826x |
| `sd6` | 7.949 s | 333.8 us | 23,816x |
| `sd6_shift1` | 22.105 s | 644.5 us | 34,297x |
| `sd7`, `hoeij_S8`, `hoeij_S9` | timeout | 1.3--24.1 ms | -- |

Miss overhead over the 368 declining rows: median 0.14 us, max 86.7 us.

The number that decides the parent issue: simulated onto the combined cactus
under the conservative budget-gated placement (the row still pays normalization
and the prime walk, both measured), the worst Hex/Isabelle cumulative ratio over
ranks 125--140 goes from **1.188x to 0.812x**, which is #9126's 0.85x criterion.
Restricting the model to the three `sd5` rows still gives 0.838x, so the
conclusion does not rest on the whole certified set. The permissive
post-normalization placement gives 0.690x. So the certificate alone would close
#9126, with the `x^n - 1` and cyclotomic rows #9126 also named untouched.

`scripts/bench/quadratic_norm_cactus_model.py` is that model, committed rather
than carried by hand: it reads the pinned records, states the replacement rule
in code, measures the retained phases per row, and leaves rows it cannot price
at their measured time.

Landed: `bench/HexBench/QuadraticNorm.lean` (bench-only, not imported by
`HexBerlekampZassenhaus`), `quadraticNormProbe` and `quadraticNormCertificate`
service entries, `scripts/bench/quadratic_norm_probe.py`,
`scripts/bench/quadratic_norm_witnesses.py`, two records, and the report.
`Hex.ZPoly.factorize` is unchanged. A fresh hex-factor sweep and regenerated
cactus and runtime-by-degree figures go with it: the new service entries put
`bench/HexBench/FactorService.lean` and `lakefile.lean` out of date against the
committed sweep, which the freshness checker treats as stale whether or not the
measured path moved.

Two things worth carrying forward. First, the independence hypothesis is not
decorative and not implied by anything weaker: `12, 20, 45` are pairwise
distinct nonsquares with `20 · 45 = 900`, and the production factorizer splits
`F(0; 12, 20, 45)` into two quartics. A certificate checking only "each `dᵢ` is a
nonsquare" would accept a reducible polynomial. Second, the paper proof uses
independence *after reordering the radicands*, so whatever shape the Lean
predicate takes it has to be usable at an arbitrary index, not only at the top
of the tower as built.

A second opinion (Codex) confirmed the theorem and found no reducible
polynomial the checker accepts, and raised five measurement findings, all now
fixed: the "budget-gated" placement was never defined and could not have meant
the existing 262,144-node recombination budget (which `sd5`'s 32,768-node walk
sits under and would never trip); the headline ratios compared spans from two
different processes; the report's `±`-monic normalization was described but not
implemented; the paired-row sweep selection was unvalidated and the witness
record carried no provenance; and one reply-count check was missing.

Three measurement traps hit on the way. Lean sinks a pure `let` to its only use,
so timing spans around pure computations read zero until each stage's result is
folded into something the reply reports; the whole certificate cost was landing
in whichever span happened to be last. And `getElem!` out of bounds does not
fail loudly -- it prints to stderr and returns the default -- so a
one-radicand input was spending 39 us formatting a panic while the driver
captured the message into a log nobody read. And the phase-profile call that
prices the model's retained phases is nearly twice as slow cold as warm on
`sd5`, which is enough on its own to move the modelled worst ratio from 0.812x
to 0.886x -- across the target. All three are fixed; the third is why the model
is a committed script that discards a warm-up call rather than a number typed
into a table.

## Current frontier

The Mathlib correspondence. The certificate is checkable and its meaning is
proved on paper; `Irreducible` is not yet a Lean theorem about it, and that is
the whole remaining cost. Decomposed into #9167 (the multiquadratic degree
theorem), #9169 (primitive element and minimal polynomial), #9168 (the
executable norm and its polynomial correspondence, plus the decidable checks),
and #9170 (production integration behind a recombination budget, with the fresh
sweep and regenerated plots).

## Next step

#9167 and #9168 are independent and can run in parallel; #9169 waits on #9167
and #9170 on both branches.

Nothing in the plan needs `axiom`, `sorry`, or `native_decide`: the check is a
`Bool` and the theorem is about it being `true`.

## Blockers

None. One premise in #9133's body did not survive contact and is recorded in the
report: the issue frames the certificate as addressing "both the combined-cactus
elbow and the `sd6` long tail", but `sd6` sits at rank 145, past the last rank
Isabelle reaches, so it contributes nothing to the elbow criterion directly. It
matters because it moves from 8.042 s to about 27.8 ms and so stops dominating
the tail -- and because the same certificate handles the `sd5` rows that *do*
sit in the elbow.
