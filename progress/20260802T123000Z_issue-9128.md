# Prime plans: scout modular degree patterns before splitting (issue #9128)

## Accomplished

The direct prime planner no longer splits candidate primes to find out how wide
they are.

- `HexBerlekamp/DegreePattern.lean` adds `DegreePattern`, `scoutDegreePattern`
  and `degreePattern?`: a bounded distinct-degree pass that separates a monic
  square-free modular image one factor degree at a time and brackets the factor
  count from both sides, abandoning the pattern once the separated factors
  reach a target and completing it otherwise.
- `directProbeScore` is refactored into `directDegreeScore`, a function of the
  prime and the degree multiset alone. That is what makes the design work: a
  complete scouted pattern scores exactly as the factorization it predicts
  would, so candidates can be compared without splitting them.
- `directPrimePlan?` splits the first good prime, scouts at most `scoutFuel`
  further good primes, and splits only the scouted winner. The fixed
  "two more full Berlekamp factorizations whenever the width exceeds 8" rule is
  gone; `scoutWidth` and `scoutFuel` remain as the hard bounds on candidates
  and work.
- `reports/hexbz-prime-scout.md` is the report, with scout/split prices per
  candidate, a five-policy offline replay, and a paired before/after.
- Fresh full Hex sweep, all 25 SVGs regenerated, three new bench records.

## Note on the first design

The first rule admitted a candidate only when its width fell by a quarter, the
materiality rule the certificate-facing selector uses. That is wrong here: a
selector comparing factorizations it has already computed loses nothing by
declining, but a scout that declines throws the candidate away. On
`hoeij_M12_f132` it discarded prime 13 (width 12) for prime 11 (width 14) and
cost 4.5x, and it dropped the lift-precision tie break on `sd6` and
`hoeij_F190` at about 10% each. Scoring the pattern directly is both simpler
and strictly better informed, and it is what landed.
