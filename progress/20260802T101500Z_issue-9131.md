# Issue #9131: canonical coefficients in the quadratic Hensel lift

## Accomplished

**Phase 1 audit, in full, with proofs.** Named the coefficient-range invariant
`Hex.ZPoly.Canonical f m` ("every coefficient lies in `[0, m)`") and proved
`reduceModPow_eq_self_of_canonical`, which is the theorem that licenses
deleting a canonicalisation rather than moving one. Proved that every field of
a quadratic step is canonical modulo the step's working modulus `m²`
(`quadraticHenselStep_canonical`), on both paths -- the word path comes free
from the existing `quadraticHenselStep_eq_bignum`.

Answered the issue's question: for **even** target exponent `k` the doubling
step's modulus squares to exactly `p^k` and the result is already canonical
there; for **odd** `k` the step overshoots to `p^(k+1)` and the descent is
genuinely required. Removed three reductions on that basis:

- `reduceLift` after an even-exponent doubling in `liftExact`;
- the closing pair in `henselLiftFactors`, same condition;
- the per-leaf reduction in `multifactorLiftQuadraticList` below the root,
  which the issue did not name -- every recursive target is a
  `henselLiftFactors` output and so canonical by construction.

Each removal is a `@[csimp]` implementation paired with the unchanged
specification function, so no existing proof about the lift changed and there
is one production algorithm, not two trees.

**The premise is right in kind and wrong in magnitude.** An arm-alternating A/B
(binaries differing only in which `@[csimp]` rules are enabled) puts the
removals at -2% to -5% of lift time on rows with many cheap lifts and at
**+0.8% / -0.0%** on `cyclo_phi179` and `cyclo_phi128_x_phi165`, the two rows
the issue targets. Lift-phase allocation, which is exact, moves by -0.03% to
-0.6%. The removals fire on the *cheapest* exponents in a chain whose cost is
geometric in the exponent.

**Located the real cost.** The baseline sampling profile's `reduceModPowImpl`
at 21.0% of total on `cyclo_phi179` is *inclusive under*
`quadraticHenselStepBignum`, not beside it: it is `reduceModSquare` inside
`addModSquare`/`subModSquare`/`mulModSquare` and, decisively, inside
`divModMonicModSquareAux`, which reduces a whole polynomial per division
iteration.

**Attacked that instead, and it pays.** `intModNatImpl` reduces through a
window: operands of those internal reductions are canonical, so their sum is in
`[0, 2m)` and their difference in `(-m, m)`. A value already in `[0, m)`
returns without allocating, `[-m, 0)` costs one bignum addition, and only
products and the doubled-precision descent still pay for `Int.emod`. Also a
`@[csimp]` on the unchanged `intModNat`.

Six alternating rounds, every round started at load average between 3.0 and
3.9, arms differing only in that one `@[csimp]` rule: lift time falls **8.7% on
`cyclo_phi179`, 10.0% on `wilkinson_56`, 12.1% on `cyclo_phi385`**, and total
factor time falls 6.4%, 6.5% and 4.7%. Exact lift allocation falls 6-9% on the
same rows. The prime-walk-bound rows `legendre_P30` and `cyclo_phi64_x_phi105`,
where `modP` reduces arbitrary rather than canonical coefficients and the extra
comparison could have cost, gain 7.3% and 9.6% instead of regressing. The only
row moving the wrong way is the 700-microsecond `chebyshev_U24` control at
+4.4% end-to-end, within its round-to-round spread.

**Conformance.** `hexhensel_emit_fixtures` and `hexbz_emit_fixtures` emit
byte-identical fixtures. The phase profile's wider mirror validation agreed
with the production trace on all 367 corpus rows. Added `#guard` coverage for
odd and even target exponents on both sides of the word-to-bignum boundary
(`k = 8, 9, 26, 27, 40` at `p = 5`, where `5^13` is the last half-exponent the
word guard admits), for canonicity of every lifted factor, and for the
factor-only last step against the projection of the full lift.

## Current frontier

`reports/hensel-canonical-lift.md` records the audit, the removals, the
windowed reduction, and where the residual sits. On `cyclo_phi179`,
`Hex.DensePoly.mulImpl` is 32.27% of total against a 72.13% lift, so
**schoolbook dense multiplication is about 45% of lift time**. That already
satisfies the trigger the issue reserves for Phase 3 ("at least 25% of material
lift time to polynomial multiplication"), before any representation rewrite.

## Next step

Two separable follow-ups, in the order the measurement supports:

1. Thresholded Karatsuba for the bignum polynomial product, proved equal to the
   schoolbook specification. At degree 89 the coefficient-multiplication count
   falls from 7921 to roughly 1100.
2. A 128-bit Montgomery word path. The word step is capped at `m * m < 2^64`,
   which on `cyclo_phi179` admits the steps up to `3^15` and excludes `3^29`
   and `3^57` -- the two expensive ones.

Phase 2's full `ResiduePolynomial` rewrite is *not* what these measurements
ask for: the reduction cost it targets is now bounded above by what the
windowed implementation leaves, and the multiplication cost it would not change
is the larger term.

## Blockers

None outstanding, but one measurement trap is worth passing on. Five attempts
at the corpus sweep read bimodally -- about a quarter of rows near 1.9x, the
slow set differing run to run -- at load averages of 2 to 5 that gave no hint
of a problem. The cause is that this corpus's recipe pins the measured service
with `taskset -c 0`, and three agent worktrees were working this tracking issue
concurrently under that recipe, so all three landed on core 0 of a 96-core
host. `ps -eo pid,psr` shows it immediately; load average does not.

The committed sweep was taken with `taskset -c 8` after checking that core and
its SMT sibling were idle, and reads median 1.001 against the baseline while
solving the same 376 rows. **When more than one measurement can run on this
host, pin each to a verified-idle core rather than to core 0.**
