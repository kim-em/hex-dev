# Multivariate Hensel lifting SPEC (issue #9322)

## Accomplished

Wrote `SPEC/Libraries/hex-mv-hensel.md` for the multivariate Hensel
lifting engine Wang's EEZ factorization needs, plus its Mathlib
companion. Registered the pair in `SPEC/Libraries/README.md` (library
list, companion list, dependency lists, DAG section, index) and pointed
the `SPEC/future-work.md` entry at the new SPEC.

The design decisions worth recording, because they are the ones a
successor would otherwise have to rediscover:

- **The prime and the lifting direction are decoupled.** hex-hensel's
  prime is simultaneously the residue field and the direction of the
  lift. Multivariately the lift moves along the evaluation ideal and the
  prime only makes univariate coefficient arithmetic invertible, so the
  working modulus `q = p^l` is fixed for the whole lift and the
  coprimality witness computed once stays valid at every step. This is
  the sharpest reason hex-hensel is a design model rather than a callable
  implementation, and it is why no hex-hensel entry point has a parameter
  in the position this library needs one.

- **The coprimality witness is the partial-fraction tuple**
  `Σ_j σ_j b_j ≡ 1 (mod q)` with `deg σ_j < deg F_j`, not pairwise
  Bézout data. It implies pairwise comaximality, it is one checkable
  identity, and combined with `p ∤ lc F_j` it also gives coprimality over
  `ℚ`, which is what makes uniqueness unconditional.

- **The leading-coefficient distribution is an input contract.** Finding
  it is recursive multivariate factorization, so it cannot live below the
  factorizer. What the lift gets from it is the degree drop
  `deg_{x_i}(f - ∏ F_j) < deg_{x_i} f`, which is exactly the hypothesis
  the degree-bounded partial-fraction map needs on its right-hand side.
  Without the contract the correction equations are unsolvable on
  correct input, so this is a load-bearing hypothesis and not a
  convenience.

- **The ideal-adic direction needs no coefficient bound.** The
  per-variable degrees of `f` bound the precision exactly, since a
  divisor cannot exceed them. Only reconstruction of integer
  coefficients needs a bound, and the two candidate bounds differ
  enormously: Kronecker substitution into hex-poly-z's Mignotte bound is
  valid but has degree `∏(d_j+1)` in the binomial factor, while
  Gel'fond's inequality is linear in `Σ d_j` in the exponent. Neither
  chooses `l` in practice; `liftWith` doubles on failure.

- **A checked lift certifies a decomposition, not irreducibility.** But
  the gap is a named theorem rather than a wall: with `f` primitive in
  the main variable and every univariate image irreducible over `ℤ`, a
  checked lift does give irreducible factors. Both extra hypotheses are
  data the factorizer holds already.

## Corrections made after review

Worth recording separately, because every one of them is a mistake the
next person writing a Hensel-shaped SPEC would make again.

- **The truncation is a box, not a power of the ideal.** Bounding each
  variable separately is congruence modulo
  `(y₁^(d₁+1), …, y_n^(d_n+1))`, and that is strictly weaker than
  congruence modulo `I^(Σd_j+1)`: at `d = (1,1)` the monomial `y₁²` is
  outside the box and inside `I³`. The containment runs one way only.
  `BoxCongr` is now the relation the algorithm's invariants use, and
  `CongrAt` survives only for genuinely ideal-adic statements.

- **Uniqueness at the base is uniqueness of residue classes.** With
  `q = 5`, `F = (x, x+1)`, and `c = 0`, both `(0,0)` and `(5,-5)` satisfy
  the degree bounds, so an equality-of-`ZPoly` conclusion is false.

- **The coefficient bound is in shifted coordinates.** The lift computes
  after translating the point to the origin, and translation amplifies:
  `x₁ + x₂²` at `x₂ = 10` has coefficients at most 1 and shifts to a
  coefficient of 20.

- **Reducing the whole factor modulo `q` destroys the exact invariants**
  when `L_j` has a coefficient outside `(-q/2, q/2]`. Only the correction
  is reduced, and it touches neither the image nor the leading
  coefficient by construction.

- **`check` alone does not give irreducibility even with primitivity and
  irreducible images.** `(x + y·x²)(x + 1)` passes every checked
  condition with `L = (y, 1)` and is reducible. `valid` is what rules it
  out, since `eval 0 y ≠ lc x`.

- The Kronecker weights omitted the main variable's radix, so the
  substitution identified `x_i` with `y₁`. And the length inequality is
  Mahler's; the height inequality usually called Gel'fond's is a
  different statement.

## Current frontier

The SPEC is complete and self-consistent. No Lean code was written; the
issue explicitly excludes implementation.

`libraries.yml` is deliberately not edited. The SPEC gives the two
entries and records that registration waits until `HexMvGcd` and
`HexModular` are registered, which is the same position hex-mv-gcd's SPEC
takes for itself.

## Next step

Implementation is milestone 1 of the seven in the SPEC: coordinates and
modulus (`shift`, `unshift`, `imageAt`, `lcIn`, `truncate`, `reduceMod`,
`SymCanonical`, `CongrAt` and their laws). That milestone touches no
factorization concept and can be built against hex-mv-poly alone, so it
does not wait for hex-mv-gcd.

## Blockers

None for the SPEC. Implementation is blocked on hex-mv-gcd and
hex-modular, both of which are specified but not yet registered or
implemented.
